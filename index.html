THE PRODUCT QUESTION

A complete lesson plan.
But is it a useful one?

The project explores a practical tension: AI can produce a polished lesson while suggesting unrealistic timing, generic inclusion strategies or assessments that miss the learning objectives.

01 / ALIGNMENT

Connect the essentials.

Ask for objectives, step-by-step activities and assessments that measure those objectives, alongside a rationale for the teaching theory.

02 / CO-DESIGN

Make priorities explicit.

Invite teachers to change the generator’s behavior using requirements such as UDL, ELL support, active learning and clearer time allocations.

03 / JUDGMENT

Question the output.

Design a complementary critique workflow. Treat an AI score as a review prompt, then use teacher expertise to assess what the tool missed.

HUMAN–AI CO-DESIGN

Let teachers choose
what better means.

Inclusion: integrate UDL principles and place ELL accommodations where they are needed.

Engagement: request active learning or gamification, then check whether the additions preserve content depth.

Usability: ask for a table, flowchart or timed activity sequence that can be followed during class.

Feasibility: question suggested digital tools, preparation requirements and time allocations against the actual classroom.

CRITIQUE MODE / EXTENSION DESIGN

Make the review visible.

The activity proposes a new tab or side-by-side review. Each criterion receives a 1–5 score with suggested improvements; educators can add or remove criteria.

REVIEW DIMENSIONTHE QUESTION IT SHOULD ASK
AlignmentDo assessments actually measure the stated learning objectives?
Cognitive loadIs the amount of information feasible within the lesson duration?
Visual clarityCan a teacher follow the lesson structure during a busy class?
Student agencyDoes the plan give students meaningful choices in how they learn?

This rubric describes proposed AI review behavior. Scores are not validated quality measurements, and no completed critique-interface build or benchmark results are provided here.
